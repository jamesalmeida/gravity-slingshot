# How sectors are made

Sectors 001–008 are handcrafted. Sectors 009–100 are generated by `tools/generate-sectors.js`, checked by a solver, and committed as plain data in `src/sectors-generated.js`. Nothing is generated while you play.

This page explains the method, so that anyone can add sectors and get the same quality.

## The idea: build the puzzle around a path that already works

The obvious way to make a level is to place bodies and relays, then search for a launch that clears it. Most random layouts have no solution, and the ones that do are often solved by a boring straight shot.

The generator works the other way round:

1. **Place the bodies** at random, with spacing rules.
2. **Fly a random launch** through them with the game's own integrator and record the path.
3. **Put the relays on that path.** The sector is now solvable by construction: the launch that drew the path clears it.
4. **Judge the result** with the gates below. Most candidates fail and are thrown away.
5. **Measure difficulty** by flying every possible launch, then pick sectors so each arc runs from easier to harder.

Steps 1–3 take a few milliseconds. Step 5 is the expensive part (57,600 flights per candidate, about two seconds), so the cheap gates run first. For each arc the generator keeps going until 40 candidates have passed every gate, then picks from those.

## What makes a sector good: the gates

A candidate is kept only if it passes all of these. Each one exists because sectors without it were bad to play.

| Gate | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Readable layout | Bodies keep their distance from each other, the launchpad and the edges. Relays sit clear of bodies, of each other and of the launchpad. Nothing covers the name printed under a body. | Overlapping labels and relays tucked behind a planet read as bugs. |
| The path stays on screen | The recorded solution must not leave the field before its last relay. | You cannot aim at what you cannot see. |
| Every relay is past a bend | The path must turn at least 18–25° between one relay and the next, and by a set amount in total. | Otherwise the relays line up and one straight shot takes them all. |
| Forgiving solution | The intended launch must still win across an unbroken range of azimuth and of speed (from 2.5° and 0.96 km/s in the first arc down to 1° and 0.32 km/s in the last). The launch is moved to the middle of that range before it is recorded. | A mouse drag moves the aim by about half a degree per pixel. A solution narrower than that is luck, not skill. It also keeps solutions valid across browsers, whose floating-point maths differ slightly. |
| No decorative wells | Remove any one body and the solution must fail. Sectors with four or more bodies may keep one that only acts as an obstacle. | A body that does nothing is clutter. |
| No straight shots | Across all 57,600 launches, the straightest winning one must still turn by a minimum amount (22° in the first arc, up to 60° in the last). | Catches layouts where a fast direct shot wins and the intended curve is optional. |
| Same route after centring | Moving the launch to the middle of its range must not turn it into a different, much longer flight. | The recorded solution, the sector's name and its brief should all describe the same path. |
| Arc theme holds | A sector in the repulsor arc must need its repulsor. A "blind" sector must have no win the predictor can show to the end. | The arc is supposed to teach that one idea. |

## Measuring difficulty

For a kept candidate the solver flies every launch on a grid of 0.5° of azimuth by 6 px/s of speed (720 × 80 launches) and records:

- **Win share**: the fraction of launches that clear the sector. This is the main measure. It is the size of the target you are searching for with the predictor.
- **Seen share**: the fraction of wins that finish within the predictor's 310 frames. When it is low, the predictor never turns emerald and you have to launch, read the ghost track of the real flight, and adjust.

They combine into one number, where one point means ten times harder:

```
difficulty = -log10( win share × (0.35 + 0.65 × seen share) )
```

The original eight sectors measure between 1.8 (Kepler Bend) and 3.2 (Triple Resonance) on this scale. The generated ones run from 1.8 (sector 009) to 3.6 (sector 100). [sectors.md](sectors.md) lists every sector's numbers.

Win share is a good measure but not a complete one. It does not know that a hairpin is harder to imagine than a gentle bend, or that five bodies are more confusing than two. That is why the campaign also changes *what kind* of sector you get, not just the number.

## The campaign: nine arcs after the original eight

Each arc adds one idea, and each has a band of win share that its sectors are picked to span, easiest first. A new arc starts a little easier than the previous one ended, so a new idea is never introduced at full difficulty.

| Sectors | Arc | New idea | Bodies | Relays | Win share, first → last |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001–008 | The Original Eight | The handcrafted originals | 1–4 | 1–3 | not ordered |
| 009–020 | Single Well | One body: speed against bend | 1 | 1–2 | 1.6% → 0.4% |
| 021–030 | Binary Systems | Two wells competing | 2 | 2 | 1.0% → 0.28% |
| 031–040 | Event Horizons | Small, heavy singularities | 2–3 | 2 | 0.8% → 0.2% |
| 041–050 | Triple Chains | Three wells, three relays | 3 | 3 | 0.55% → 0.14% |
| 051–060 | Repulsor Fields | Repulsors, which push | 2–3 | 2 | 0.7% → 0.16% |
| 061–070 | Narrow Beacons | Relays at 70% radius | 2–3 | 2–3 | 0.36% → 0.09% |
| 071–080 | Blind Transfers | Last relay beyond the predictor | 2–3 | 2 | 0.5% → 0.1% |
| 081–090 | Crowded Skies | Four and five bodies | 4–5 | 3 | 0.26% → 0.07% |
| 091–100 | Grand Tours | All of it together | 4–5 | 3–4 | 0.14% → 0.035% |

Within an arc the generator also varies the shape of the intended path. Each candidate is asked for a gentle bend, a hairpin or a loop, and when two candidates fit a difficulty slot equally well, the selector takes the shape the arc has used least.

The bands are targets. The selector takes the closest candidate available, so the real numbers in `docs/sectors.md` wander around them.

## Regenerating

```sh
npm run generate        # rebuild src/sectors-generated.js and docs/sectors.md (about 7 minutes on 10 cores)
npm run check           # re-fly every recorded solution (about a second; this is what CI runs)
npm run check:sweep     # full sweep of all 100 sectors (about a minute)
```

The generator is seeded (`--seed`, default 2026). The same seed gives the same sectors on the same version of Node; a different JavaScript engine can round a few flights differently and pick different candidates. That is why the output is committed rather than rebuilt in CI, and why CI checks the committed sectors instead.

To change the campaign, edit the `ARCS` table at the top of `tools/generate-sectors.js`: body line-ups, relay count and size, how far the path must turn, how forgiving the solution must be, and the win-share band.

## Adding a sector by hand

1. Add it to `src/sectors.js` with a `probe`, `bodies` and `relays`. The first body must attract, because the Pe / Ap readout measures from it.
2. Run `node tools/check-levels.js --sweep --sector N`. It reports the share of winning launches and the most forgiving one.
3. Record that launch as `solution: { angle, speed }` (screen degrees, px/s) so the fast check can re-fly it.
4. Aim for a solution that forgives at least 1° and 0.3 km/s, and check that the straightest winning launch is not a straight line.

## Should sectors be generated while you play?

Not for the campaign. Generating ahead of time and committing the result means every player gets the same 100 sectors, each one has been through the full sweep, and a bad sector can be looked at and replaced. The sweep is what makes the quality reliable, and at about two seconds per candidate it is too slow to run in the browser for every candidate.

An endless or daily mode is a reasonable later addition. Steps 1–3 and the cheap gates run in well under a second, so the browser could build a solvable, forgiving sector from a seed (for example, today's date). What it could not do cheaply is grade it: such a sector would be guaranteed solvable but its difficulty would be a guess.
